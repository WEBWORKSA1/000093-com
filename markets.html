China Markets 101

How China's four major exchanges fit together, what the boards mean, and the routes global investors use. Written for people new to these markets.

SSE · Shanghai

Shanghai Stock Exchange

Home of large state-linked companies, banks, energy and consumer giants (Main Board, 60x codes), plus the tech-focused STAR Market (688) launched in 2019.

SZSE · Shenzhen

Shenzhen Stock Exchange

Leans toward private companies and growth sectors. It runs the Main Board (000–003) and ChiNext (300), the growth board where registration-based IPOs began in 2020.

BSE · Beijing

Beijing Stock Exchange

Opened in 2021 for innovative small and medium-sized firms. It has a wider ±30% daily band, and new codes use the 920 prefix.

HKEX · Hong Kong

Hong Kong Exchanges

An international market that trades in HKD. It has no daily price limits and uses 5-digit codes. Many mainland companies list here as "H-shares" or "red chips".

Stock Connect

Northbound lets international investors buy eligible Shanghai and Shenzhen shares through Hong Kong. Southbound lets mainland investors buy eligible HK shares.

QFII / RQFII

A licensed channel that lets qualified foreign institutions invest directly in onshore securities.

ETFs & ADRs

The simplest route for most retail investors is a global ETF tracking the CSI 300 or MSCI China, or US-listed shares of Chinese companies.

A股 A-shareOnshore yuan-denominated shares traded in Shanghai, Shenzhen or Beijing.
H股 H-shareShares of a mainland-incorporated company listed in Hong Kong.
红筹 Red chipA company incorporated offshore with its main business in mainland China, listed in Hong Kong.
ST / *ST"Special treatment": a warning label for financially troubled companies, with a narrower ±5% limit.
